HeurChain vs Mem0 on LoCoMo

Mem0 leads by nine answers out of 543, and temporal questions account for all of it. A trace of HeurChain's misses shows why: it hands the answerer relative dates such as "yesterday" and never resolves them.

What the data shows

  • Temporal decides the result. Mem0's +18 on temporal is twice the overall gap, and Mem0 leads or ties on temporal in all four DEV conversations. If HeurChain matched Mem0 there and changed nothing else, it would lead by 9.
  • The temporal gap is an answer-side problem, not a retrieval one. Every labeled evidence turn reached HeurChain's context for 106 of 122 temporal questions. 26 of its 46 misses come from relative dates that were never resolved, and Mem0 answered 19 of those correctly.
  • Multi-hop is a retrieval problem. All labeled evidence reached HeurChain's context for only 30 of 106 multi-hop questions. 46 of its 56 multi-hop misses were missing at least one evidence turn.
  • The overall gap is not established. 143 questions split between the systems: 67 went only to HeurChain and 76 only to Mem0. Conversation 41 contributes +13 for Mem0; without it, HeurChain leads by 4.
  • Two published values are not usable targets. The published Overall doesn't follow from the paper's own category scores, and the open-domain column may not cover the same questions.

Why HeurChain misses temporal questions

We traced all 46 temporal questions HeurChain missed back to the evidence it handed Luna, using only the frozen DEV artifacts and no model calls. Both arms used the same answer prompt, which says to answer UNKNOWN when the evidence is insufficient. The difference is what each memory put in front of that prompt.

Why the published columns are context only

  • The published Overall doesn't follow from the paper's category scores. Weighting Table 1 by LoCoMo's category sizes (841 / 282 / 321 / 96) gives 62.1% for Mem0 and 61.4% for Mem0-Graph, not 66.88% and 68.44%. The order of the two also flips. Don't use 68.44% as a bar.
  • The category mapping is unpublished. Mem0 never stated which LoCoMo category number carries which label (mem0ai/mem0#2609), and at least one other team shipped transposed labels before retracting a claim (widemem). Local Mem0 lands within 3–7 points of its published single-hop and multi-hop scores but 51.5 points below its published open-domain score.
  • Local Mem0 is not the paper's system. It is frozen Mem0 OSS v3 with Luna as extractor, answerer and judge. The paper used its 2025 architecture, GPT-4o-mini and a ten-run judge average. Those differences are the likely reason local Mem0 beats its paper on temporal by 21.5 points, and the matched report makes no claim against the published scores.

Research directions

Ranked by answers at stake. The answer prompt is shared by both arms in the matched protocol, so a HeurChain improvement has to change what HeurChain stores or retrieves, not the prompt.

Temporal

Mem0 +18 · need +19

The diagnostic found that retrieval is mostly fine (106 of 122 questions had all evidence in context). The misses come from relative time (26), date-scoped retrieval (6), other retrieval misses (5), arithmetic (4), other answer errors (3) and gold labels (2).

Build, in order

  1. Resolve relative time when storing. Detect "yesterday", "last week", "last year" and similar phrases in each turn, compute the date or range from the session timestamp, and store it beside the raw words, for example "[yesterday = 24 Aug 2023]". Targets the 26 relative-time misses, 19 of which Mem0 answered. TReMu, TSM and Chronos all do a version of this.
  2. Index the resolved date. Keep an event-date field separate from the session date, and match it against dates named in the question. Targets the 6 date-scoped retrieval misses. LongMemEval calls this time-aware query expansion.
  3. Do date arithmetic in code. Route durations and "how long between" questions to a deterministic date calculation. Targets 4 misses that Mem0 also missed, so this is where HeurChain can pass it.
  4. Protect dates in any summary step. If consolidation is turned back on, instruct it to keep dates. The Sleeping Agent found that gist compression drops dates; one added prompt sentence raised date preservation from 3% to 62%.
  5. Report the two gold-label problems (conv-26-qa-005, conv-41-qa-048) and score them both ways.

Multi-hop

Mem0 +7 · need +8

All labeled evidence reached HeurChain's context for 30 of 106 multi-hop questions, some for 54 and none for 21 (one has no labels). HeurChain answered 20 of 30 with full evidence, 25 of 54 with partial and 4 of 21 with none. The 16 KB cap never cut evidence; the 20-unit limit binds.

Build

  • A gap-driven second pass. After the first retrieval, name the missing fact or entity and query again. EviMem reports 85.2% on LoCoMo multi-hop this way, against 65.9% for MIRIX; IRCoT is the older template.
  • Entity expansion on the lexical path. Take names from the top-ranked turns and re-query on them. In ProGraph, removing this kind of expansion cost 22.6 points on its multi-hop benchmark.
  • Bigger units, same budget. Index segments of a few adjacent turns so each of the 20 slots carries more context (SeCom).
  • Test every change against single-hop.

Single-hop: protect it

HeurChain +13 · budget 12

HeurChain leads in three of four conversations, most likely because it keeps raw turns where Mem0 keeps extracted facts that can drop detail. Add resolved dates beside the raw text; don't replace it. Gate every change on single-hop staying at 213 / 287 or above. There is headroom too: 39 of HeurChain's 62 single-hop misses had no labeled evidence in context.

Open-domain: low priority

HeurChain +3 · 28 questions

Both systems are weak here (21–32%), and 19 of the 28 questions defeated both. These LoCoMo questions mostly ask for an inference about a person. One question moves the score 3.6 points, so don't tune on this slice.

Evaluation practice

  • Keep DEV and holdout separate. These 543 questions are the four DEV conversations. Diagnose and iterate here, then confirm once on the six sealed holdout conversations (997 category 1–4 questions).
  • Report direction per conversation. Four conversations are too few for a population claim, so the holdout carries any generalization.
  • Re-grade split questions with an independent judge. Luna answered and graded both arms in one pass. The historical rubric counts an answer correct if it "refers to the same date or time period", which makes temporal grading lenient and phrase-sensitive.
  • Keep the arms matched. Same answer prompt, top-20 limit and 16 KB budget. Change only what each memory stores and retrieves.
